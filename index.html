What's going on?

NYC Parks raised almost $10 million to expand and reconstruct the kids' pool at Tompkins Square Park. Money is so abundant NYC Parks officials decided to redo the calisthenics park, the basketball courts, a shaded courtyard and nearby areas. This plan closes everything for two years or more.

Who asked for this?

Nobody. More than 2,500 3,000 3,500 people have signed a petition asking NYC Parks to remove the calisthenics park from the scope of this project and keep the space accessible during the pool reconstruction. We also want NYC Parks to minimize disruption to basketball players.

Council Member Harvey Epstein and Assembly Member Keith Powers have endorsed these requests. We expect that Senator-elect Grace Lee will join them soon.

Did NYC Parks listen to the community?

Not really. Again and again, and as shown by their own records, Parks recorded unanimous feedback in favor of keeping the courts and calisthenics park untouched. But then, at the first CB3 meeting, they argued forcefully for demolishing and rebuilding everything anyway.

What's so special about these bars and courts?

Residents and park users have a lot to say.

  • The bars are high-quality, rock-solid, perfectly-sized, versatile and fully accessible.
  • The bars and courts nurture a vibrant, diverse, inclusive community.
  • They are historic, and well-known among calisthenics athletes everywhere.
  • Bars and courts are free, and used year-round by people of all ages.

What does NYC Parks say?

NYC Parks officials have claimed that the equipment is broken, expired, non-compliant with code, and non-accessible to people with disabilities. They also claim that the area poses a liability risk to the city, and that it sits atop a clogged drainage pipe.

The evidence for these claims, however, is severely lacking. As all users can attest, the bars are rock-solid, and Parks itself admits it has not investigated their structural integrity. The “code” that the bars allegedly infringe (ASTM F3101) is a private voluntary and optional standard. The area is regularly used by people with disabilities or rehabilitating from injury. It is also fully accessible to strollers and wheelchairs (we see them there). Liability is a reasonable concern when the city is buying new equipment that has never been used. These bars have been in use for nearly 40 years. Why did Parks decide to care about this alleged risk now?

Are you guys just weirdo-NIMBY-types?

No. We have no objection to an improved pool for kids, and no objection to climate resilience. We are not opposing the building of new housing either, as that's not even on the agenda. We want to retain an open public space that works. That's why we want CB3 to endorse NYC Parks' original Option 3, which removes the calisthenics park from the scope of the project, with modifications to minimize disruption to basketball players, and to ensure the area remains accessible, as it has always been.

So maybe we are KIMBY: Keep It in My Back Yard.

Let's get a better plan

  • Respect the people who use the park. It does not count as listening if you don't listen
  • Upgrade the pool
  • Repair the courts and the calisthenics surface
  • Keep the courtyard open, so delivery workers have a place to rest
  • Make the area more accessible
  • Never close everything at once
  • Preserve this area as an epicenter of wellbeing, affordability and community

We are not arguing that nothing ever needs repair. We are arguing that Parks is misrepresenting the status of the park and using repairable problems to justify a much larger, more disruptive reconstruction. The result will be a city that is less affordable, less equal and less inclusive.
