What's going on?

NYC Parks raised almost $10 million to expand and reconstruct the kids' pool at Tompkins Square Park. Money is so abundant NYC Parks officials decided to redo the calisthenics park, the basketball courts, a shaded courtyard and nearby areas. This plan closes everything for two years or more.

Who asked for this?

Nobody asked for this. On the contrary, more than 2,500 3,000 3,500 people have signed a petition asking NYC Parks to remove the calisthenics park from the scope of this project and keep the space accessible during the pool reconstruction. We also want NYC Parks to minimize disruption to basketball players.

Council Member Harvey Epstein and Assembly Member Keith Powers have endorsed these requests. We expect that Senator-elect Grace Lee will join them soon.

Did NYC Parks listen to the community?

Not really. Again and again, and as shown by their own records, Parks recorded unanimous community feedback in favor of keeping the courts and calisthenics park untouched. But then, at the first CB3 meeting, NYC Parks officials argued forcefully for demolishing and rebuilding everything anyway.

What does NYC Parks say?

Now that NYC Parks has money to spend, its staffers decided that the Tompkins bars are broken, expired, non-compliant and non-accessible. They also claim that the area poses a liability risk to the city, and that it sits atop a clogged drainage pipe. The evidence they provide for these claims, however, is slim or absent. And as we show below, NYC Parks’ own records tell a very different story.

1. Durability

Parks itself admits it has not investigated the structural integrity of the bars. Anyone who visits the site, sees how the bars are used, or tries to give them a vigorous shake can attest they are rock-solid.

Even NYC Parks’ own records agree. Parks inspectors checked Tompkins 61 times from May 2000 to August 2026, and Parks staff wrote 3,458 work orders for the park from January 2010 to October 2026. In all that paper trail, Parks never recorded serious damage to the bars. At most, it shows one weld in 2010, which Parks rated as low priority. The bars’ only other problems, worn paint and a worn surface, went away when Parks did basic maintenance. That makes them some of the sturdiest, most dependable outdoor fitness equipment in the city.

2. Accessibility

The area is accessible and is used by people with disabilities or rehabilitating from injury, and by people with strollers, crutches and wheelchairs (we see them there). This is an open-ended calisthenics park, not a weight room where machines have seats, movable weights, hinges and cables that dictate how one should use the equipment. At Tompkins, people adapt the bars to their bodies and levels of mobility. Read our note on accessibility →

Parks’ own records point the same way. When inspectors wrote up the rubber surface under the bars, in 2017 and 2019, they described it as an “accessible area.” And starting in 2008, its inspectors regularly recorded an accessible sign in the park, the last time in January 2019.

3. Compliance

The “code” that the bars allegedly infringe (ASTM F3101) is actually a private voluntary and optional standard (read the footnote). Unlike children's playgrounds, where IPEMA offers independent certification, in the outdoor fitness industry manufacturers self-certify.

4. Liability

Parks says the bars are a liability risk. But risk comes from equipment that fails, and these bars have been in use for nearly 40 years. Such a long record is tangible evidence that the risk is low. New equipment has no record at all. Replacing proven bars with untested ones increases risk instead of reducing it.

5. Drainage

Parks has not shared its pipe inspections. And it told CB3 it could run a new drain line while “minimally disrupting the existing fitness.” So why demolish everything on top?

NYC Parks officials also said that stormwater detention tanks tend to go under basketball courts. NYC keeps data on detention tanks, and NYC Parks’ arguments are not substantiated by the official records. See our note on detention tanks →

At Tompkins, new will most likely be a downgrade

New equipment is not just unknown. Where Parks has installed it, its own records show it is a downgrade. We checked the same kinds of records for 64 other parks with outdoor adult fitness areas. At 40 of them, including many newly renovated ones, Parks found serious, chronic or premature problems. The new equipment comes loose, gets wobbly, or breaks down surprisingly early and surprisingly often.

Maybe you guys are some weirdo NIMBY types?

No. We have no objection to an improved pool for kids, and no objection to climate resilience. We are not opposing the building of new housing either, as that's not even on the agenda. We want to retain an open public space that works. That's why we want CB3 to endorse NYC Parks' original Option 3, which removes the calisthenics park from the scope of the project, with modifications to minimize disruption to basketball players, and to ensure the area remains accessible, as it has always been.

So maybe we are KIMBY: Keep It in My Back Yard.

Let's get a better plan

  • Upgrade the pool
  • Increase climate resilience
  • Remove the bars from the scope of the project, as NYC Parks has already offered to do
  • Minimize disruption to basketball players
  • Keep the city affordable, accessible and inclusive
