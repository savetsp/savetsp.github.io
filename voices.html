Voices from the park

From park users and petition signers, in their own words. Read the petition.

Said to CB3, October 27

“I represent the trenches, the unheard and the people called undesirable at the last meeting. We are not against refreshing the park. We're against closing it for 18 months… These aren't just pull up bars. They're therapy, recovery, community and family. We lost enough of the neighborhood. 18 months is too long.”

Jahmal
Read the full testimony

My name is Jahmal Kerwin. I'm a lifelong Lower east side resident. Avenue D to be exact. I'm representing Baruch Houses, Lillian Wald, Jacob Riis, Campos Plaza and every other projects in the Lower east side. My mother moved here at 6. She's 80 now. My father came here as an immigrant escaping poverty. I represent the trenches, the unheard and the people called undesirable at the last meeting. We are not against refreshing the park. We're against closing it for 18 months. Two years ago, I was on the brink of death. Drinking every night, partying every day, hospitalized multiple times. Tompkins Square Park and the FDR workout area helped save my life. I watched my neighborhood change without us having any say. Today me and my NLB crew provide free workouts. Artists like Theophilus London work out alongside everyday residents. People coming home from prison, kids aspiring to be athletes, and people of all ages. Nobody gets judged. Everybody belongs to now the F, now the FDR. Hold on. Now the FDR is closing through 2027 and you want to close Tompkins too? Where are we supposed to go? Why not finish the FDR first? I'm scared of losing the place that helped me recover. The place that changed my life. These aren't just pull up bars. They're therapy, recovery, community and family. We lost enough of the neighborhood. 18 Months is too long. Please don't take away the one last thing we have left.

I'm a lifetime New Yorker, grew up on the Upper east side as well as hell's kitchen. I'm 46 years old and I've seen the way the city has changed year after year. Written for CB3, October 27

“The Tompkins Bars embody all the values I want for him. It's a shared public space open to everyone. It's diverse. The place is overflowing with mutual support. I've never seen anywhere else like it in all my time in NYC.”

Joe
Read the full statement

I'm a journalist, East Village resident, and a father of two.

I love living in NYC. Sometimes people ask me what it's like raising kids in the city or the East Village. And I always say it's the best.

Sure sometimes I think it'd be nice to have a backyard, a place to grill, a place to play catch. But it's fine because we have our parks, and Tompkins Square has been a special place for both of our children for a decade now.

My son is 7 years old, and he's everything I wished I could've been at that age, but never could be: He's cool, confident, athletic, charismatic, and strong. He recently got it in his head that he wants to be a jacked bro with a six pack. And I think that's awesome.

So on his own, starting early this year, he started gravitating to the exercise bars anytime we'd take him to the playground. Being a responsible parent, I figured I should follow him there. And now I try to take him basically every day after school.

Of course I'm thrilled to see how strong he's getting. Stronger than I'll ever be. But that's not the real reason I care so much about saving the bars.

The Tompkins Bars embody all the values I want for him. It's a shared public space open to everyone. It's diverse. The place is overflowing with mutual support. I've never seen anywhere else like it in all my time in NYC.

If the bars go away, sure I'll find another place for him to exercise. But the community there, which has been so valuable for us and so many others will be obliterated for good.

It's great we have some cash to make Tompkins an even better place. But let's not destroy an offline community, at a time when it's never been more necessary or harder to find

“I am 73 years old, and I have used the monkey bars in Tompkins Square Park since they were installed… Their design fosters communal interaction and brings a diverse cross-section of people from the neighborhood and the City to enjoy the park as their playground.”

Sally
Read the full statement

I am 73 years old, and I have used the monkey bars in Tompkins Square Park since they were installed to keep me in shape and prevent the back problem that kept me in bed for weeks from happening again.  To support keeping the bars, I have two points. First, the current ones and those installed in other parks offer two different experiences. The current ones let users explore their own body language as they use the bars and discover ways to improve their stretch, alignment, balance, and strength in ways best aligned to their goals and long-term health. Their design fosters communal interaction and brings a diverse cross-section of people from the neighborhood and the City to enjoy the park as their playground. They are called “monkey bars” because people can play on them in ways they can't on gym equipment. Stationary equipment takes the fun out of using your body because it's designed to tell users how they must be used and limits use to one person at a time. They isolate people rather than bring people together. Second, this shouldn’t be an either/or situation. Can’t the park find space to leave the bars alone and install stationary equipment in suitable places so everyone can choose the apparatus that works for them and enjoy both designs?

“I have visited thousands of public spaces around the world that inspire movement and that Tompkins, and specifically the bars and courts at 10+B stand out as world class… It shouldn't be considered for redesign it should be held up as an example city wide for what we want to see.”

Jesse
Read the full statement

I'm not educated traditionally, I don't know more than any of you in your field of expertise, I'm sure, and there are considerations I am no doubt ignoring.

What I can say is I have visited thousands of public spaces around the world that inspire movement and that Tompkins, and specifically the bars and courts at 10+B stand out as world class. The amount of people, all ages, year round at all times of day, that use this small corner of a park is bar none.

I've been training parkour for twenty two years and I run the movement creative, we've been doing parkour and movement programming with the city and in schools and out around in parks for the last fifteen years.

Tompkins has taught me a lot over the years, I've trained martial arts and calisthenics and parkour here with friends from all around the world. I've seen people do things I thought were impossible, and then spent the time to learn how to do them to.

But I'm not here for parkour.

That there is a creative playground that adults can use without the company of a child makes Tompkins nearly one of a kind in New York City.

What I'm here for is to promote intergenerational movement and play and sport in public space. Diversity breeds immunity, if you roll your ankle in basketball you can still get a calisthenics workout in, if your age or interest or ability changes you have the path laid before you and you have examples in your community of people who have found interesting passionate ways of staying healthy and active. That you can't go in a playground after you're thirteen is a major messaging and public space issue in New York City. Across America this idea is threatening individual and community health. Movement is a human right and creative and diverse movement in public space is something prevalent in other citys in the country and other countries, and it's strikingly, dangerously rare in New York.

Tompkins is the medicine for that problem.

It shouldn't be considered for redesign it should be held up as an example city wide for what we want to see.

A place where people hang out, work out, play ball, and can monkey around is the gold standard.

I've seen the redesigns, I understand there is a lot of money on the table, I know a repair bill, while smaller, probably comes from a different budget, and most importantly

I don't question the intentions of the people on this board to create a better park.

I don't know what brilliance inspired this park design in the first place but I consider that sacred wisdom.

What I do question is what gets lost when two years are cut out of a community's heart, what we lose when we go from one of a kind to cookie cutter, and whether the thousands of people who know and love and use the park regularly right now are the ones who benefit from the changes. I know if Tompkins gets torn down it will feel like the east River amphitheater redone without the bandshell, it will feel like the M Paul Friedberg beam and bar playgrounds getting replaced with sterile replicas,

it will feel like one of my best friends passed away.

I dropped out of high school, I don't know anything about how this process works, I'm intimidated, frustrated, and upset, I'd love to stand here and ask you to make ten more parks in the spirit of Tompkins 10+B, but instead I'm here before you, humble and unqualified to say - please for the love of movement - keep the bars the way they are.

Keep the bars the way they are.
