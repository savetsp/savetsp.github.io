More mischaracterizations

More of what Parks isn't telling you, from its own documents and its October 15 presentation to CB3. The experts Parks engaged want to make the project as large as they possibly can. They misunderstood what they saw and created a presentation full of mischaracterizations, all in tiny font, so CB3 would agree with them.

  • The money. The $6.1 million state grant can only go to the pool. "We use it for the pool or we lose it," Parks told CB3. So City Hall added $3.83 million of city money, as Parks put it, "because we can't just do a pool." That is city money spent demolishing courts and a calisthenics park that work.
  • The options. Parks presented three inadequate plans. CB3's Parks Committee chose the worst one. Why close everything at once, and demolish, when they could repair and maintain?
  • The landmark. Parks' own analysis (slide 58) lists the pros of the current equipment, and they are concrete and specific: "NYC Parks calisthenics landmark," "Strong community support for it to remain as is," "Unique in the outdoor fitness community." The document also lists the cons, and they are a sad excuse for Parks to ignore New Yorkers and waste our money: "Built in late 1980's," "Painted multiple times," "peeling paint & missing bolts." Repair it, as Parks has done so many times before. Why rip it all up just because you can?
  • The "same" equipment. Parks told CB3 the bars would be "faithfully recreated in kind." Its own analysis says otherwise. Every option (see slide 58) changes them in important ways, and the only one that comes close is flagged as "highest overall cost," with "no warranty on field welded parts." The current setup is tested by its users every day, and they vouch for its outstanding quality. If cost is a concern, repair and maintain, instead of demolishing and building it back worse.
  • The "flex space." Parks keeps calling a new "flex space" an improvement. The whole area is already flex. People train, play, stretch, jump rope, rest and meet friends there every day.

Our request to CB3: vote no on all three options, and ask Parks to propose a plan that emphasizes repair and maintenance instead of demolition, and improves accessibility while minimizing disruption to users.
